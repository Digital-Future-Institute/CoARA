**Preprints** are complete but not yet published research papers which are shared online before completing formal peer review. They are deposited in open access servers to allow for speedy dissemination of findings.  

Because the publishing process can be slow, this also provides authors with the opportunity to gain early feedback.


## Why do people share pre-prints?

The immediate sharing of results is particularly important in the scientific community, as findings can influence ongoing research without waiting for formal publication.

The publishing process can be very slow, and this strategy avoids data becoming out of date before it is in print.

Pre-prints also:

- Promote **transparency** by making research more open  
- Allow the research community to see **methods, data, and analysis** early  
- Provide a more **democratic approach** to knowledge sharing  
- Avoid journal paywalls, making research **accessible** to a wider audience

## What are the drawbacks?

One of the biggest drawbacks is that pre-prints are **not yet peer reviewed**, meaning the claims have not been verified by experts in the field.

This can be a concern because:

- They may contain methodological flaws  
- Analyses may be incomplete or incorrect  
- Misinformation can spread if findings are misinterpreted  

There is also a potential risk for researchers: posting a pre-print shares your ideas publicly, and competitors may attempt to publish similar work before you do.


## If I want to publish a pre-print, how do I safeguard it?

It is important to use an appropriate and reputable server, such as:

- **Preprints.org** – https://www.preprints.org/

This platform offers quality screening services that help demonstrate research credibility.

You can also safeguard yourself by:

- Uploading updated versions as the work evolves  
- Including a **changelog** so readers can see what has changed  
- Adding a clear **disclaimer**, for example:

> *This work is a preprint and has not yet undergone peer review. Therefore, findings and analysis should be considered preliminary.*

## Selected examples of preprint servers

- **bioRxiv** (life sciences) – https://www.biorxiv.org/  
- **medRxiv** (medicine and health) – https://www.medrxiv.org/  
- **PsyArXiv** (behavioral sciences) – https://psyarxiv.com/  
- **SocArXiv** (social sciences and humanities) – https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv  
- **arXiv** (physics, mathematics, computer science) – https://arxiv.org/  
- **ChemRxiv** (chemistry) – https://chemrxiv.org/
