## Introduction

According to [LIBER EU](https://libereurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/LIBER-FAIR-Data.pdf), the [FAIR Data Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAIR_data) are a set of guiding principles aimed at making data **Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable**. They provide guidance for scientific data management and stewardship relevant to all stakeholders in the current digital ecosystem, encouraging data producers and publishers to promote maximum use of research data.

For [OpenAIRE](https://www.openaire.eu/how-to-make-your-data-fair) the FAIR principles are necessary to increase the availability of online resources. Data should be created with **longevity in mind**, giving other researchers access to facilitate knowledge discovery and improve research transparency. The principles describe how research outputs should be organised so they can be more easily accessed, understood, exchanged, and reused. Major funding bodies, including the [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/isa2/actions/measuring-maturity-data_en/), promote FAIR data to maximise the integrity and impact of research investment.


## EC FAIR Data Recommendations

The EU proposes [six Recommendations for Implementation of FAIR practice](https://op.europa.eu/en-GB/publication-detail/-/publication/4630fa57-1348-11eb-9a54-01aa75ed71a1/language-en):

1. Fund awareness-raising, training, education, and community-specific support.  
2. Fund development, adoption, and maintenance of community standards, tools, and infrastructure.  
3. Incentivise development of community governance.  
4. Translate FAIR guidelines for other digital objects.  
5. Reward and recognise improvements in FAIR practice.  
6. Develop and monitor adequate policies for FAIR data and research objects.

## 1. Making Your Data Findable

A dataset is **findable** when others can identify, locate, and understand its key characteristics. This begins at project planning and continues through to publication.

### Key Practices

- **Use persistent identifiers (PIDs)** such as DOIs for datasets, ORCID IDs for authors, and grant identifiers where appropriate. Many repositories assign DOIs automatically.  
- **Create rich metadata** including:
  - Creator(s)  
  - Data content  
  - Methods used  
  - Temporal and geographic details  
  - Instrumentation or textual sources  
- **Metadata standards guidance**: [OpenAIRE How to Make Your Data FAIR](https://www.openaire.eu/how-to-make-your-data-fair)  
- **Choose appropriate repositories** that support discipline-specific metadata and indexing:  
  - Humanities: Archaeology Data Service, Zenodo, Figshare, institutional repositories  
  - Biosciences: EMBL-EBI, GenBank, ArrayExpress  
  - STEM: GitHub + Zenodo, ICPSR  
- **Use meaningful file names and project structures** for both humans and machines.  
- **Training and further guidance**: [Durham University FAIR Guide](https://libguides.durham.ac.uk/open_research/fair)  


## 2. Making Your Data Accessible

Accessibility concerns **clarity of access**, not whether the data are open.

### Key Practices

- Metadata should remain openly accessible, even when data are confidential or restricted.  
- Document **access protocols**: authentication, request processes, reuse conditions, embargo periods.  
- Use standardised, open communication protocols (HTTP, HTTPS, FTP, APIs).  
- Apply clear licences:
  - Humanities & social sciences: Creative Commons  
  - Software & models: domain-specific licences  
  - [Introduction to Open Licences Guide](https://github.com/javieraatenas-pixel/testOSC/wiki/A-Practical-Guide-for-Understanding-the-FAIR-Principles)  
- Practical guidance: [The Turing Way FAIR Practices](https://book.the-turing-way.org/reproducible-research/rdm/rdm-fair/)  

## 3. Making Your Data Interoperable

Interoperability enables data from different sources, instruments, or contexts to be **combined and understood** across systems and disciplines.

### Key Practices

- Use **standard, documented file formats** (CSV, XML, JSON, HDF5, TEI-XML, WAV, TIFF). Avoid proprietary formats where possible.  
- Adopt **controlled vocabularies, ontologies, and metadata schemas** relevant to your field:  
  - Health: SNOMED CT, ICD  
  - Life sciences: Gene Ontology  
  - Humanities: Dublin Core, TEI Ontologies  
  - Social sciences: Data Documentation Initiative (DDI)  
  - Engineering: ISO standards  
- Reference domain standards: [GO FAIR](https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/) and [Pistoia Alliance](https://fairtoolkit.pistoiaalliance.org/fair-guiding-principles/)  
- Document data transformations and cleaning procedures.  
- Link datasets using **PIDs** to support cross-referencing.  


## 4. Making Your Data Reusable

Reusability relies on high-quality documentation, licences, provenance information, and adherence to ethical standards.

### Key Practices

- Provide detailed documentation:
  - Research questions  
  - Experimental protocols  
  - Codebooks or dictionaries  
  - Workflows and scripts  
  - Limitations and quality assessments  
- Apply **explicit licences**: [Introduction to Open Licences Guide](https://github.com/javieraatenas-pixel/testOSC/wiki/A-Practical-Guide-for-Understanding-the-FAIR-Principles)  
- Describe **provenance**: collection, processing, transformations, versions, corrections  
- Ensure compliance with ethical and legal frameworks  
- Use version control for code and dynamic datasets  
- Examples and guidance: [Open Science EU FAIR Guide](https://www.openscience.eu/article/infrastructure/guide-fair-principles)  


## 5. A Practical Roadmap for Becoming FAIR

| Stage | FAIR-Aligned Actions |
|-------|--------------------|
| Project Design | Draft a Data Management Plan referencing FAIR; choose repositories; identify sensitive data early. |
| Data Collection | Record consistent metadata; choose interoperable formats; document instruments and workflows. |
| Data Processing | Maintain clear version control; log transformations; create codebooks. |
| Data Analysis | Use reproducible workflows; store code and scripts; link outputs with PIDs. |
| Publication & Sharing | Deposit data and metadata; assign licences; ensure metadata remain public even if data are restricted. |
| Post-Project Stewardship | Monitor metadata quality; maintain PIDs; update licensing or embargoes when necessary. |

Step-by-step guidance: [OpenAIRE How to Make Your Data FAIR](https://www.openaire.eu/how-to-make-your-data-fair)  


## 6. How PIs Can Support FAIR Implementation

PIs play a crucial role in establishing group-wide practices and ensuring compliance with funder expectations.

### Leadership Actions

- Integrate FAIR into project governance and staff training  
- Allocate time and resources for data management roles  
- Encourage use of institutional or disciplinary repositories  
- Promote transparent documentation and open standards  
- Ensure compliance with ethical approvals, GDPR, and cultural stewardship  

The FAIR principles align with broader open science initiatives such as **DORA** and **COARA**, which emphasise responsible research assessment and knowledge sharing.
