## Open Access

According to [UNESCO. (2026). *Why OA: Open access*.](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000397811.locale=en) Open access (OA) makes research outputs freely available for everyone to access and responsibly reuse. Guided by the [UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science (2021)](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000383771), it promotes transparency, equity, collaboration, and trust in science while recognising ethical, legal, and social responsibilities. Open access respects human rights, privacy, intellectual property, Indigenous and local knowledge, security, and bioethical considerations, ensuring that scientific knowledge is shared for the benefit of society within appropriate ethical boundaries.

OA promotes equitable access to knowledge by making research available to a wider global audience. It encourages thoughtful and responsible sharing of research outputs, including data, while broadening participation to educators, policymakers, young people, civil society, and others beyond academia. By increasing accessibility and inclusivity, OA strengthens collaboration, trust, research diversity, and reproducibility [(UNESCO, 2026)](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000397811.locale=en) 


## Open Access Models

Open access (OA) models provide a framework for making research outputs freely available and clarify where specific costs are allocated. There are various options including **Green**, **Gold**, **Diamond**, **Bronze**, and **Hybrid** models.

![Types of Open Access](https://www.openaccess.nl/sites/default/files/inline-images/20241217_types_OA.jpg)


## Gold Open Access

Gold open access means that an article is immediately free to read on a publisher's website.

This usually involves an **Article Processing Charge (APC)** paid by the funder, author, or institution. The final publication is openly available with an open licence (such as Creative Commons).

APCs can vary widely depending on the journal and publisher. Costs often reflect factors such as:

- Publishing and editorial processes  
- Production costs  
- Pricing strategies  

Typical costs range from a few hundred pounds to several thousand pounds.

This model may be useful if:
- Funders require immediate open access  
- Your institution has an open access publishing agreement  

However, for individual authors, cost is often a major consideration.


## Green Open Access

Green open access is often known as **self-archiving**.

In this model:
- The work is published in a journal.
- A version of the manuscript is deposited in a repository.

This route is free for authors but may involve an **embargo period** before the manuscript can be made publicly available.


## Diamond Open Access

Diamond Open Access means that work is **free to read and free to publish**.

Unlike the Gold model, Diamond OA is non-commercial and is often managed by academic organisations or scholarly societies. There are **no APCs**.

This model is widely recognised as an equitable approach to scholarly publishing and aligns closely with UNESCOÔÇÖs 2021 Recommendation on Open Science, which promotes knowledge as a digital public good.

Examples include:

- **Open Library of Humanities (OLH)** Humanities and cultural studies  
- **Language Science Press** Linguistics  

## Bronze Open Access

Bronze open access articles are free to read on a publisher website but lack a clear open licence.

This means:
- Reuse rights are unclear  
- Access may be temporary  
- It is often used as a promotional tool  

This is not considered a sustainable form of open access, as some content may later move behind a paywall.

## Hybrid Open Access

Hybrid open access is linked to subscription journals that offer an optional open access route for individual articles.

Authors can:
- Pay an APC to make their specific article open access  
- Publish in a journal where the remaining content remains behind a paywall  

# Why Does This Matter?

Open science is built on **sharing, transparency, and collaboration**.

![Benefits of Open Access](https://openeconomics.zbw.eu/wp-content/uploads/1967/04/OpenAccess-Reasons.png)

Choosing the right open access model helps researchers:

- Share knowledge widely  
- Comply with funder requirements  
- Maintain transparency  
- Support equitable access to scholarship  

Importantly, open science does not mean researchers lose their intellectual rights. An ecosystem of licences, norms, and scholarly practices ensures that intellectual contributions are protected while enabling openness.

Here is an example of how open access benefits learners: [Walled Garden](https://tecfaetu.unige.ch/perso/maltt/kouchek1/P6/WalledGardenBeta/Game) developed by students to raise awareness about the locking in of proprietary software used in universities.  Students developed this game within a course in the Master of Science in Learning and Teaching Technologies to describe the student experience when having to do an academic work and stumble into paywalls (in French).
