## Introduction 
Open Science and **Research Assessment Reform (RAR)** are interconnected movements that aim to improve the  **quality, integrity, transparency, and societal value** of research. Historically, research assessment systems have relied heavily on publication **counts, journal prestige, citation metrics, and institutional rankings**. However, these measures often fail to recognise the full range of contributions that support [excellent research](https://www.scienceeurope.org/our-resources/position-statement-os-ra/), including data sharing, software development, collaboration, public engagement, mentoring, and [open research practices](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reforming-research-assessment-for-better-science_f6202159-en/full-report.html)

International initiatives led by **[Science Europe](https://www.scienceeurope.org/our-resources/position-statement-os-ra/), [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/open-science/inclusive-science/collaborative-action-research-assessment-context-open-science-coara), the [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reforming-research-assessment-for-better-science_f6202159-en/full-report.html), and [CoARA](https://www.coara.org/)** emphasise that Open Science and Research Assessment Reform should be pursued together. Open Science cannot become the norm if assessment systems fail to recognise and reward open practices, while assessment reform depends on greater transparency and openness throughout the research lifecycle.

For researchers, this means that excellence is increasingly being understood not simply in terms of outputs, but also through research quality, integrity, openness, collaboration, and [societal contribution](https://www.scienceeurope.org/our-resources/position-statement-os-ra/)

## Open Science, Assessment Reform, and Research Culture

Both Open Science and Research Assessment Reform seek to reshape research culture by aligning incentives with the behaviours and practices that support high-quality research. Together, they encourage a shift from competitive, output-driven systems towards more collaborative, inclusive, and responsible approaches to research.

In practice, this means creating environments that:

- **Reward** quality, rigour, and integrity rather than quantity alone.
- **Support** collaboration, teamwork, and knowledge sharing.
- **Encourage** transparency, reproducibility, and openness throughout the research lifecycle.
- **Recognise** diverse career pathways, expertise, and contributions.
- **Value** data stewardship, software development, mentoring, engagement, and leadership alongside publications.
- **Promote** equity, inclusion, and responsible research practices.
- **Strengthen** public trust in research and research institutions.
- **Enable** research to generate benefits for both academic communities and society more broadly.

Ultimately, Open Science and Research Assessment Reform share a common vision: that research excellence should be defined not only by outputs, but by the quality of research practices, the openness of research processes, the **diversity of contributions** that support discovery, and the positive impact that research can have on society. By embedding these principles throughout the research lifecycle, researchers can contribute to a more transparent, inclusive, and sustainable research system.

### Connecting Open Science and Research Assessment Reform

| Open Science Principle | Research Assessment Reform Principle | Shared Goal |
|------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------------|
| Open access to publications | Recognition of diverse outputs | Improve access to knowledge |
| FAIR and open research data | Recognition of datasets as research outputs | Increase reuse and transparency |
| Open software and code sharing | Recognition of software contributions | Support reproducibility |
| Open methodologies and workflows | Assessment of research quality rather than venue prestige | Improve reliability |
| Citizen science and public engagement | Recognition of societal contributions | Enhance public value |
| Open collaboration | Recognition of team science | Support cooperative research cultures |
| Open peer review and transparency | Responsible use of qualitative assessment | Increase trust and accountability |


### Why Reform Research Assessment?

Research assessment influences:

- Funding decisions;
- Hiring and promotion;
- Career progression;
- Institutional reputation;
- Research priorities.

Traditional approaches can unintentionally encourage:

- Quantity over quality;
- Competition over collaboration;
- Closed research practices;
- Selective reporting;
- Limited recognition of non-publication outputs.

Reform seeks to create assessment systems that better reflect how high-quality research is actually conducted in contemporary research environments. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/reforming-research-assessment-for-better-science_f6202159-en/full-report.html); [Research Data Alliance](https://www.rd-alliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2022_06_20_2022_RDA_research_assessment.pdf) and [Science Europe](https://www.scienceeurope.org/our-resources/position-statement-os-ra/)

### What Counts as Research Contribution?

A key principle of Research Assessment Reform is recognising a broader range of contributions.

| Traditional Recognition | Expanded Recognition |
|-------------------------|---------------------|
| Journal articles | Publications, datasets, software, protocols, educational resources |
| Citation counts | Research quality, openness, and integrity |
| Individual achievement | Team science and collaborative contributions |
| Prestigious journals | Diverse dissemination routes |
| Research outputs | Research processes and practices |
| Academic impact | Societal, cultural, policy, and economic contributions |

Researchers should therefore document the full scope of their contributions throughout a project.

### Open Science Practices that Support Assessment Reform

Researchers can align their work with both Open Science and responsible assessment by:

| Area | Recommended Practices |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Research Design** | Pre-registration, transparent methods, documented workflows |
| **Data** | FAIR data management, data sharing, metadata creation |
| **Software** | Open-source code, version control, software citation |
| **Publication** | Open access publishing and preprints where appropriate |
| **Collaboration** | Interdisciplinary and international collaboration |
| **Engagement** | Citizen science, stakeholder participation, public engagement |
| **Research Culture** | Mentoring, leadership, peer review, community-building |


### Indicators of Research Quality

Research Assessment Reform emphasises evaluating the quality of research practice rather than relying solely on publication metrics.

| Characteristic | Examples |
|---------------|----------|
| Quality and Rigour | Robust methodology and transparent reporting |
| Transparency | Open methods, data, code, and documentation |
| Integrity | Ethical conduct and responsible research practices |
| Reproducibility | Clearly documented processes and workflows |
| Collaboration | Effective teamwork and interdisciplinary engagement |
| Inclusivity | Diverse participation and equitable research practices |
| Societal Value | Contributions beyond academia |

### Examples and Best Practices

One of the key goals of Open Science and Research Assessment Reform is to ensure that researchers are recognised not only for what they publish, but also for how they conduct, share, and contribute to research. In practice, this means moving beyond narrow measures of success and demonstrating a broader range of scholarly activities and outputs.

| Research Activity | Traditional Assessment | Open Science and RRA-Aligned Practice |
|-------------------|-----------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Publishing research findings | Journal articles only | Open access articles, preprints, registered reports, and open peer review |
| Managing research data | Data retained privately | FAIR datasets deposited in trusted repositories with documentation |
| Software development | Little formal recognition | Open-source software, code repositories, documentation, and software citation |
| Research methods | Methods described briefly in publications | Open workflows, protocols, notebooks, and reproducible methodologies |
| Collaboration | Focus on lead authorship | Recognition of team science, interdisciplinary and international collaboration |
| Public engagement | Optional activity | Co-creation, citizen science, stakeholder engagement, and knowledge exchange |
| Research leadership | Principal investigator responsibilities | Mentoring, supervision, capacity building, and fostering positive research culture |


### Best Practices for Researchers

Researchers can strengthen their alignment with Open Science and Research Assessment Reform by adopting the following practices throughout the research lifecycle.

| Area | Best Practice |
|--------|--------------|
| **Research Planning** | Build openness, transparency, and reproducibility into project design from the outset. |
| **Data Management** | Create Data Management Plans and apply FAIR principles throughout the project. |
| **Documentation** | Produce clear metadata, data dictionaries, codebooks, and methodological documentation. |
| **Sharing Outputs** | Share data, software, protocols, and publications where ethical and legal requirements permit. |
| **Collaboration** | Recognise diverse contributions and support team-based approaches to research. |
| **Engagement** | Involve relevant communities, stakeholders, and users where appropriate. |
| **Research Integrity** | Be transparent about methods, limitations, assumptions, and uncertainties. |
| **Professional Development** | Document mentoring, leadership, training, service, and community-building activities. |


- Example 1: Traditional vs Reformed Assessment

**Traditional Approach**

- Published three journal articles.
- Assessed primarily on publication venue and citation metrics.
- Limited recognition of data, software, mentoring, or engagement activities.

**Open Science and RRA Approach**

- Published two open-access articles.
- Deposited a FAIR dataset in a repository.
- Released analysis code through GitHub.
- Mentored postgraduate researchers.
- Delivered public engagement activities with stakeholders.
- Shared methods and documentation to support reproducibility.

Under a reformed assessment framework, all of these contributions contribute to the overall evaluation of research quality and impact.

- Example 2: Demonstrating Open Research Practice

Instead of stating:

> "The project will generate high-impact publications."

A stronger statement might be:

> "The project will publish findings through open-access channels where possible, deposit supporting datasets and code in trusted repositories, engage relevant stakeholders throughout the research process, and provide clear documentation to support reuse and reproducibility."

This demonstrates openness, transparency, and a commitment to maximising the value of research outputs.

- Example 3: Recognising Diverse Contributions

| Contribution | How it May Be Evidenced |
|-------------|-------------------------|
| Data Stewardship | FAIR datasets, metadata, repository deposits |
| Software Development | GitHub repositories, software documentation, releases |
| Research Culture | Mentoring, supervision, training activities |
| Open Science Leadership | Open research advocacy, training delivery, policy development |
| Public Engagement | Citizen science, workshops, community partnerships |
| Collaboration | Interdisciplinary projects, international partnerships |
| Methodological Innovation | New workflows, tools, protocols, or analytical approaches |


- Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Avoid | Better Practice |
|---------|---------------|
| Measuring success solely through publication counts. | Demonstrate a broad range of research contributions. |
| Treating data and software as secondary outputs. | Recognise and document them as valuable research outputs. |
| Focusing only on individual achievements. | Highlight collaboration, teamwork, and shared contributions. |
| Sharing outputs without adequate documentation. | Provide metadata, licences, and supporting documentation. |
| Making unrealistic impact claims. | Present clear and credible pathways to impact. |
| Viewing Open Science as an optional add-on. | Embed openness throughout the research lifecycle. |

Researchers do not need to transform every project into a fully open project. Rather, they should aim to make research as open, transparent, reproducible, and reusable as possible while respecting ethical, legal, cultural, and disciplinary requirements. Open Science and Research Assessment Reform are most effective when they are integrated into everyday research practice and recognised as part of high-quality, responsible research.

### Embedding Reform Within Research Projects

Open Science and Research Assessment Reform should not be viewed as separate policy initiatives or compliance exercises. Rather, they provide a framework for designing, conducting, and evaluating research in ways that promote quality, transparency, integrity, collaboration, and societal value. Embedding these principles from the earliest stages of project development helps researchers create more robust, reproducible, and impactful research while ensuring that a wider range of contributions can be recognised and valued.

Researchers should consider how openness, transparency, collaboration, and responsible assessment can be integrated throughout the research lifecycle rather than being addressed only at the point of publication. By doing so, projects are better positioned to demonstrate not only what was achieved, but also how research was conducted and who contributed to its success.

| Project Stage | Questions to Consider |
|--------------|----------------------|
| **Planning** | How will openness, transparency, collaboration, and responsible research practices be embedded from the outset? What contributions beyond publications might emerge from the project? |
| **Data Collection** | How will research data be documented, managed, preserved, and shared responsibly? How will participants, communities, and stakeholders be considered throughout the process? |
| **Analysis** | How will methods, workflows, software, and decisions be recorded to support transparency, reproducibility, and future reuse? |
| **Dissemination** | Which outputs will be produced beyond traditional publications, such as datasets, software, protocols, training materials, policy briefs, or public-facing resources? |
| **Impact** | How will the project engage with relevant communities, users, policymakers, industry partners, or the public? How might benefits emerge over time? |
| **Evaluation** | How will diverse contributions, collaborative activities, mentorship, data stewardship, software development, and open research practices be recognised and evidenced? |

## Moving Beyond Metrics

A central objective of Research Assessment Reform is to move away from narrow measures of excellence based primarily on publication counts, citation rates, journal prestige, and institutional rankings. While quantitative indicators can provide useful information, they should support rather than replace expert judgement.

Good research cannot be reduced to a single metric. Research quality is better understood through a combination of scholarly rigour, openness, integrity, collaboration, innovation, and contribution to knowledge and society.

| Traditional Approaches | Responsible Assessment Approaches |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------|
| Using Journal Impact Factor as a proxy for quality | Evaluating the quality, rigour, originality, and relevance of the research itself |
| Assessing researchers primarily through publication counts | Recognising a diversity of outputs, contributions, and career paths |
| Narrow definitions of impact based solely on citations | Considering scholarly, societal, cultural, educational, environmental, and policy impacts |
| Prioritising individual achievement | Recognising team science, interdisciplinary collaboration, and collective contributions |
| Judging quality through institutional or journal prestige | Using evidence-based evaluation of research processes, outputs, and outcomes |
| Rewarding quantity of outputs | Rewarding integrity, openness, transparency, and reproducibility |
| Focusing only on publications | Recognising datasets, software, methods, infrastructure, engagement, mentoring, leadership, and capacity building |

Researchers should therefore focus on demonstrating the value and quality of their contributions rather than relying on prestige indicators or numerical measures alone.
