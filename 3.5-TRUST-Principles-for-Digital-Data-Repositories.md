## The TRUST Principles

The **TRUST Principles** provide a framework for ensuring that digital repositories are **reliable, sustainable, and worthy of user confidence over time**. Originally articulated in *Scientific Data* (Lin et al., 2020), these principles focus on the organisational and infrastructural conditions required to support long-term data stewardship.

In contrast to the **FAIR principles** which focus on making data Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable  **the TRUST Principles** emphasise the **governance, management, and operational practices** that ensure data remains usable and trustworthy in the long term. Together, FAIR and TRUST are complementary: **FAIR defines what good data looks like, while TRUST defines how repositories must operate to support that data sustainably**.

### Definition of TRUST

The acronym **TRUST** stands for:

- **Transparency**: Repositories should provide clear information about their mission, scope, policies, and services so users understand how data is managed.  
- **Responsibility**: Repositories must take responsibility for the integrity and authenticity of data, ensuring proper curation and stewardship.  
- **User Focus**: Services should be designed with the needs of users in mind, supporting accessibility, usability, and community engagement.  
- **Sustainability**: Long-term preservation and access must be ensured through stable funding, governance, and strategic planning.  
- **Technology**: Infrastructure should be robust, secure, and interoperable, using appropriate standards and practices to support ongoing access and reuse.  

These principles help ensure that data repositories are not only technically capable but also **institutionally reliable and ethically governed**.

### Relationship to Open Science

Within **open science**, TRUST plays a critical role by ensuring that open data is not only shared, but also **maintained, preserved, and usable over time**. Without trustworthy repositories, FAIR data cannot be effectively sustained or reused.

## The Five TRUST Principles: Definitions and Best Practices

**Transparency, Responsibility, User Focus, Sustainability, Technology**

The TRUST Principles complement the FAIR and CARE Principles by focusing on the trustworthiness of digital repositories and the long-term stewardship of research data.

| TRUST Principle | Purpose | Key Commitments | Best Practices / Examples |
|----------------|---------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **T  Transparency** | Ensure repositories provide clear, publicly accessible information about their mission, governance, policies, and services. | Clear mission and scope<br> Open policies and procedures<br> Public governance and funding information<br> Clear terms of use and licensing<br> Regular reporting |  Publish mission statement and collection scope<br> Make deposit, access, and preservation policies available<br> Document metadata standards and curation workflows<br> Specify preservation timeframes<br> Publish fee structures and licensing conditions<br> Disclose governance arrangements and funding models<br> Provide public service reports |
| **R  Responsibility** | Demonstrate accountable stewardship of data holdings and fulfil ethical, legal, and technical obligations. |  Data integrity and authenticity<br> Compliance with legal and ethical requirements<br> Persistent identification and citation support<br> Management of sensitive data<br> Accountability mechanisms |  Implement documented curation procedures<br> Apply recognised metadata standards<br> Assign persistent identifiers (e.g., DOIs)<br> Maintain version control policies<br> Provide secure access for restricted data<br> Ensure compliance with data protection legislation<br> Enable correction, withdrawal, or retraction processes |
| **U  User Focus** | Align repository services with the needs, norms, and expectations of designated communities. |  Community engagement<br> User support and training<br> Accessible discovery and access services<br> Continuous improvement through feedback<br> Interoperability with research infrastructures |  Adopt discipline-specific metadata schemas and ontologies<br> Provide guidance, help services, and training materials<br> Offer intuitive search and download interfaces<br> Establish feedback channels and advisory boards<br> Support interoperability across repositories<br> Monitor usage metrics to improve services |
| **S  Sustainability** | Ensure long-term preservation, service continuity, and organisational resilience. |  Long-term planning<br> Risk management<br> Preservation strategies<br> Financial and organisational resilience<br> Continuous evaluation and improvement |  Maintain governance structures and succession plans<br> Develop disaster recovery and business continuity plans<br> Diversify funding sources<br> Conduct periodic risk assessments<br> Implement preservation strategies (migration, replication, fixity checks)<br> Pursue certifications such as CoreTrustSeal or ISO 16363 |
| **T  Technology** | Provide robust technical infrastructure for secure storage, preservation, interoperability, and reliable access. |  Secure and reliable systems<br> Data integrity monitoring<br> Authentication and authorisation controls<br> Backup and replication mechanisms<br> Interoperability support<br> Cybersecurity management |  Use well-maintained, standards-based platforms<br> Perform checksum and fixity validation<br> Maintain secure authentication systems<br> Implement geographically distributed backups<br> Support APIs and protocols such as OAI-PMH<br> Monitor and address cybersecurity risks through regular updates |


## TRUST, FAIR, and Certification

The TRUST Principles are complementary to FAIR and provide the structural conditions necessary for FAIR data to remain usable over time.

In practical terms:

- **FAIR** addresses the qualities of data objects.  
- **TRUST** addresses the qualities of the repository as an institution.  

Repositories may demonstrate alignment with TRUST through external certification, such as:

- CoreTrustSeal  
- ISO 16363 (Trusted Digital Repository standard)  

Certification provides external validation of repository practices and enhances confidence among funders and research communities.

## Implications for Policy and Institutional Practice

For universities, funders, and research organisations, TRUST offers a practical evaluative framework:

- **Repository Selection**: Researchers should deposit data in repositories that demonstrably adhere to TRUST principles.  
- **Policy Development**: Institutional data policies can incorporate TRUST criteria when approving or recommending repositories.  
- **Research Assessment**: Funders and publishers may use TRUST alignment as an indicator of appropriate data stewardship.  
- **Capacity Building**: Investment in repository governance, preservation expertise, and technical infrastructure strengthens research ecosystems.  

## TRUST principles and digital stewardship

The TRUST Principles articulate a holistic understanding of digital stewardship that integrates governance, ethics, technology, and community engagement. Trustworthiness is not merely a technical property but a socio-technical achievement grounded in accountability and long-term commitment.

In an era of increasing emphasis on open science and data reuse, TRUST provides a principled framework through which repositories can demonstrate reliability, and through which researchers and institutions can make informed, responsible choices about data preservation and access.
