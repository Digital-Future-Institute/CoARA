## Implementing the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance


The **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** provide guidance for ethical, equitable, and rights-based data practices involving Indigenous Peoples.

CARE complements the **FAIR Principles** by centering **people, purpose, rights, and relationships** in data governance.

Indigenous Data Sovereignty is grounded in the [United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP)](https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2018/11/UNDRIP_E_web.pdf), affirming Indigenous Peoples rights to control data about their peoples, lands, territories, resources, and knowledge systems.

CARE applies to:

- **Indigenous Knowledge and Traditional Knowledge**  
- **Scientific data involving Indigenous lands, waters, peoples, or resources**  
- **Metadata, genomic data, environmental data, and digital collections**  
- **Historical and contemporary datasets**  

## The CARE Principles

| CARE Principle | Purpose | Key Commitments | Examples / Applications |
|----------------|---------|----------------|--------------------------|
| **C Collective Benefit** | Ensure that data ecosystems generate benefits for Indigenous Peoples and communities. |  Support inclusive development and innovation<br> Improve governance and citizen engagement<br> Enable equitable outcomes<br> Align data use with community values<br> Strengthen Indigenous nations and communities | Data initiatives that support community priorities, strengthen local governance, and create social, cultural, environmental, or economic benefits for Indigenous Peoples. |
| **A Authority to Control** | Recognise and empower Indigenous Peoples rights and authority over Indigenous data. |  Recognise Indigenous rights in data governance<br> Support Indigenous-led decision-making<br> Enable community-defined access protocols<br> Ensure accurate and respectful representation of Indigenous lands, resources, and knowledges<br> Facilitate access to data for governance and self-determination | Indigenous governance frameworks, community-controlled data repositories, culturally appropriate access protocols, and Indigenous-led stewardship models. |
| **R Responsibility** | Ensure that those using Indigenous data act responsibly and support Indigenous self-determination. | Transparent reporting of data use<br> Capacity building and data capability development<br> Strengthen Indigenous languages and cultures<br> Maintain accountability through documentation<br> Engage ethically with communities | Community partnerships, training initiatives, data stewardship plans, public accountability mechanisms, and ongoing consultation processes. |
| **E Ethics** | Prioritise Indigenous rights, wellbeing, and values throughout the data lifecycle. |  Minimise harm<br> Maximise benefit<br> Promote justice and equity<br> Respect Indigenous ethical frameworks<br> Support culturally appropriate reuse over time | Ethical review processes, culturally responsive research practices, community consent mechanisms, and long-term stewardship that respects Indigenous values and traditions. |

**CARE Pathway:**

**Collective Benefit Authority to Control Responsibility and Ethics**

The CARE Principles complement the FAIR Principles by ensuring that data governance is not only technically effective but also socially just, culturally respectful, and aligned with Indigenous rights and self-determination.


## CARE and FAIR: Complementary Frameworks

FAIR promotes technical data stewardship and open science.  
CARE ensures that openness aligns with Indigenous rights and values.

CARE implementation ensures that:

- Data is **as open as determined by Indigenous communities**
- Data reuse is **purposeful and equitable**
- Open data does not override Indigenous governance
- Cultural metadata and provenance are included
- Historical power imbalances are addressed

CARE is a required dimension of responsible open science.

## Applying CARE Across the Data Lifecycle

CARE principles apply across all stages of research data management.

| Research Stage | CARE-Aligned Actions |
|---------------|---------------------|
| **Planning Stage** |  Engage Indigenous communities early<br> Identify whether data involves Indigenous peoples, lands, or knowledge<br> Establish governance agreements<br> Define benefit-sharing mechanisms<br> Document consent and community protocols<br> Plan for cultural metadata and provenance tracking |
| **Data Collection** |  Follow community-approved methodologies<br> Respect cultural protocols<br> Ensure informed and ongoing consent<br> Clarify data ownership and authority<br> Avoid extractive practices |
| **Data Processing & Documentation** |  Include cultural metadata<br> Record Indigenous provenance<br> Apply community-defined classification systems<br> Use tools such as Traditional Knowledge (TK) Labels where appropriate<br> Document intended use and restrictions |
| **Storage & Access** |  Implement access controls aligned with community decisions<br> Distinguish between open, restricted, and community-only data<br> Embed governance rules in metadata<br> Ensure security and sovereignty where possible |
| **Data Sharing & Reuse** |  Require ethical review for secondary use<br> Respect community-determined sharing conditions<br> Track reuse and derivative works<br> Ensure attribution and acknowledgement of Indigenous authority<br> Evaluate benefit-sharing outcomes |
| **Long-Term Stewardship** |  Maintain community engagement<br> Support ongoing governance oversight<br> Update permissions as contexts change<br> Ensure intergenerational benefit |


### Traditional Knowledge (TK) Labels

TK Labels are digital metadata tags that:

- Restore Indigenous cultural authority
- Improve provenance documentation
- Embed community protocols in metadata
- Support culturally appropriate reuse
- Work within existing catalogues and digital infrastructures

TK Labels demonstrate how CARE and FAIR can operate together.
